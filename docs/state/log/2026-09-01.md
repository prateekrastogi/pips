The question is not "does this work on average". A strategy that made everything in one regime
and nothing since has an excellent average and no future. Three design choices, each mirroring
one already made elsewhere:

- **Worst era, not the mean** — averaging is the one summary that cannot see a regime-dependent
  edge, because the good era's contribution survives being divided. Same reasoning as the
  concentration leave-outs.
- **A single era cannot be tested**, and not-testable is not a pass — the stress battery's third
  outcome. Splitting one era into "eras" and reporting stability reads the same as a genuine
  multi-era pass, which is the objection.
- **Eras are GIVEN, not discovered.** Choosing a boundary after seeing the returns fits the
  split to the answer — the ex-post selection A05/A06 are pre-registered apart to prevent.

An era with too few observations is reported as **unmeasured** rather than scored or dropped:
scoring it makes the verdict swing on noise, dropping it silently shrinks the test. An
unmeasured era blocks stability exactly as a failing one does — neither has been shown to work.
Returns belonging to no era are counted too, for the same reason the ratio check reports its
skips.
